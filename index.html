Why Children May Need an Orthodontic Evaluation

Many parents assume that orthodontic treatment should begin only when all permanent teeth have erupted. However, an early evaluation can help identify developing problems with tooth eruption, jaw growth, crowding, and bite alignment.

The American Association of Orthodontists commonly recommends that children have an orthodontic checkup around age seven. This does not mean every child will need braces at that age. Instead, an early examination gives an orthodontist an opportunity to monitor development and determine whether early treatment may be beneficial.

At Westwood Orthodontics, children can receive an individualized assessment to determine whether treatment is needed or whether continued observation is appropriate.

Signs Your Child May Need an Orthodontist

Parents may want to schedule an orthodontic evaluation if they notice signs such as:

  • Crowded or overlapping teeth
  • Large spaces between teeth
  • Teeth that appear to be erupting incorrectly
  • An overbite or underbite
  • A crossbite
  • Protruding front teeth
  • Early or late loss of baby teeth
  • Difficulty biting or chewing
  • A jaw that shifts when opening or closing
  • A narrow upper dental arch

These signs do not automatically mean that a child requires orthodontic treatment. A professional evaluation can help determine the cause and appropriate next steps.

Early Orthodontic Treatment for Children

Early orthodontic treatment, sometimes called Phase 1 orthodontics or interceptive orthodontics, focuses on children who are still growing and developing. Depending on the child's individual development and orthodontic needs, early treatment may be recommended during childhood.

The purpose of early treatment is not necessarily to complete the entire orthodontic process immediately. Instead, treatment may help address certain bite problems, create space for permanent teeth, or improve the conditions for future orthodontic treatment.

In some cases, children may receive early treatment followed by a monitoring period while their remaining permanent teeth erupt.

Braces for Kids in Los Angeles

Traditional braces remain an effective orthodontic option for children. Modern braces are designed to provide controlled tooth movement while addressing concerns such as crowding, spacing, and bite alignment.

At Westwood Orthodontics, braces are among the treatment options available for children and teens. Treatment is customized according to the child's age, dental development, orthodontic concerns, and individual needs.

Invisalign First for Growing Children

Some children may also be candidates for Invisalign First, a clear aligner system designed for younger patients. Clear aligners can be removable for eating and brushing and have a less noticeable appearance than traditional braces.

However, not every child is automatically a candidate. An orthodontist should evaluate the child's dental development, treatment requirements, and ability to follow aligner-wearing instructions before recommending this approach.

What Happens During a Child's Orthodontic Visit?

A children's orthodontic consultation should be informative and comfortable for both the child and parents. During an evaluation, the orthodontic team may examine tooth positioning, jaw development, bite relationships, and the eruption of permanent teeth.

Digital imaging and other diagnostic records may be used when appropriate to better understand the child's developing smile. Parents can also use the consultation to ask questions about treatment timing, braces, clear aligners, oral hygiene, appointments, and expected treatment stages.

Supporting Your Child's Smile at Home

Orthodontic treatment works best when children maintain good oral hygiene and follow their orthodontist's instructions. Parents can help by encouraging children to brush carefully around brackets and wires when they have braces and to keep regular dental cleaning appointments.

Children using removable aligners should follow the orthodontist's instructions for wearing, cleaning, and storing their aligners. Avoiding foods that can damage braces and attending scheduled orthodontic appointments can also help treatment progress as planned.

Most importantly, parents should contact the orthodontic office if an appliance becomes damaged or if their child experiences an orthodontic concern.
